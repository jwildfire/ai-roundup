AI in Clinical Trials digest — Monday, September 21

New since Thursday, September 17. Five items.

Stanford's 37,000-agent "virtual biotech" mines 56,000 trials, independently converges on a validated drug target

A multi-agent system with divisions mimicking a real drug company catalogued outcomes across 55,984 Phase II/III trials in about six hours and proposed a lung-cancer antibody-drug conjugate that a major pharma company later landed on independently — a concrete, published (Science) proof point for agentic AI as an R&D research workforce.

Stanford Medicine, Sept 17 2026

TrialAtlas multi-agent system outperforms OpenAI and Gemini deep-research on FDA rejection letters

A purpose-built multi-agent architecture, benchmarked against 291 real FDA Complete Response Letters, detects trial design deficiencies and predicts regulatory success with 85%+ balanced accuracy — a rare case of agentic AI evaluated directly against FDA outcomes rather than a generic leaderboard.

arXiv, Sept 18 2026

AI imaging endpoint caught a survival benefit in a Phase 3 lung-cancer trial that RECIST missed

Altis Labs' IPRO model, run on ~10,000 CT scans from J&J's MARIPOSA trial, flagged a favorable treatment effect from week 16 onward, while standard RECIST-based response rate failed to anticipate the eventual 12+ month overall-survival benefit — a citable case for AI-derived imaging endpoints replacing or augmenting classic response criteria.

Clinical Trials Arena, Sept 17 2026

New eClinicalMedicine review maps AI evidence gaps against ICH E6(R3)/E9(R1) and FDA/EMA frameworks

A state-of-the-evidence review lays out where AI in RBQM and pharmacovigilance signal detection helps versus where it risks inflating query volume without improving safety, and ties recommendations directly to ICH and EU AI Act/EMA guidance — a ready-made reference slide for the regulatory-landscape section of the talk.

The Lancet eClinicalMedicine, Sept 19 2026

AI algorithm steers real-time formulation and dosing decisions inside a live clinical trial

Quotient Sciences reports a proprietary AI algorithm selecting modified-release formulation compositions and doses during an ongoing trial, hitting the target pharmacokinetic profile within three dosing periods — a concrete example of AI making adaptive calls inside patient dosing, not just at the planning or monitoring stage.
